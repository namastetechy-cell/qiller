Why does notebook paper have a red line down the side, and three holes punched in it?
Start with the red line. That's the margin.
It keeps your writing away from the edge, where pages fray, tear, and get dog-eared.
And it leaves room for something every student dreads: the teacher's notes.
It's red so it stands out against the light blue lines you actually write on.
Now the holes. Much of the world punches just two.
America went with three, spaced 4¼ inches apart, to fit the three-ring binder.
Three rings hold a letter-size page at the top, middle, and bottom, so it doesn't flop or rip out.
