Why is the school bus almost always yellow?
Blame a meeting in 1939.
Back then, American school buses came in every color, some even red, white, and blue.
So professor Frank Cyr gathered experts at Columbia University to agree on one standard.
They hung paint samples on a wall and picked a bright yellow-orange.
It became National School Bus Yellow.
Why that shade? It stands out at dawn and dusk, in rain and fog.
Your eyes catch yellow fast, even from the corner of your eye.
And black letters on yellow are easy to read from far away.
Many countries copied the idea, so yellow now means: kids on board, slow down.

Fact notes: in 1939 Frank W. Cyr (Teachers College, Columbia University) convened a national conference on school-bus standards;
paint samples were hung on a wall and a yellow-orange was chosen — first called "National School Bus Chrome", today "National School
Bus Glossy Yellow". Chosen for visibility in early-morning and dusk light and for black lettering contrast. Yellow is used in the US,
Canada and many other countries, but not everywhere — the script says "many countries", not "worldwide".
