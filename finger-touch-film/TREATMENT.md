# Why touchscreens feel your finger but not your pen — treatment

## Three candidate structures
1. **A proof.** Claim "fingers work, pens don't" at the top; the board fills downward, each step boxed. Clear, but static for 35 s vertical.
2. **A debate.** Two pens, two halves of the board: finger side vs pen side, drawings meet in the middle. Good for comparison, but the mechanism (charge under the glass) gets split in two.
3. **Into the glass and back out (chosen).** Start on a phone drawn on the board: a pen tap gets "?", a finger tap gets a ripple. The camera *pushes through the screen* into a magnified cross-section where the mechanism lives, then pulls back out to the phone for the ending, where a stylus with a conductive tip finally gets its ripple. The vertical frame suits a tall board: phone at the top, the magnified glass below, the camera dives *down* the board like going under the surface.

Why: the question is "what's happening under the glass"; the camera literally goes there. The ending bookends the opening tap and resolves the pen's failure.

## Logline and arc
A pen taps a phone and is ignored; we dive under the glass to see the charge grid that only a conductive finger can disturb — and come back up to give the pen a conductive tip that finally works.
- Setup (0–5 s): pen tap → "?"; finger tap → ripple. "Why?"
- Turn (5–23 s): under the glass: grid holds charge; finger = salty water; charge magnets slide off the grid into the fingertip; the grid feels the dip → a target circle.
- Contrast (23–29 s): the plastic pen over the same grid — the magnets don't move. "insulator" written, charges stay. Near-silence before it.
- Ending (29–35 s): pull back up to the phone; conductive tip drawn on the pen; tap → ripple + check mark, circled twice, pen capped.
