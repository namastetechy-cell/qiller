[surprised] Ever feel like you're not that smart? Here are five signs you're selling yourself short.
[sad] One. You doubt yourself. Research suggests skilled people often underestimate how good they are.
[happy] Two. You're curious. A mind that keeps asking why tends to keep learning.
[surprised] Three. You talk to yourself. Saying a word out loud can help you focus, and find things faster.
[happy] Four. You can say, I don't know. Intellectual humility goes hand in hand with learning.
[cool] Five. You're funny. Studies link quick humor to verbal intelligence.

Fact notes (claims are hedged: "suggests", "tends to", "linked")
- 1 Doubt: Kruger & Dunning (1999): top performers tended to underestimate their relative standing, while the least skilled overestimated.
- 2 Curiosity: von Stumm, Hell & Chamorro-Premuzic (2011), "The hungry mind": intellectual curiosity predicts academic performance
  alongside intelligence and conscientiousness.
- 3 Self-talk: Lupyan & Swingley (2012): saying an object's name aloud helped people find it faster in visual search.
- 4 "I don't know": Leary et al. (2017), Krumrei-Mancuso et al. (2020): intellectual humility is associated with openness to
  learning and greater knowledge acquisition.
- 5 Humor: Greengross & Miller (2011): humor-production ability correlated with verbal intelligence.
