Ever noticed the tiny hole in your ballpoint pen's cap? It's not there to keep the ink fresh.
It's there in case someone swallows the cap.
Lots of us chew on pen caps. Every so often, one slips down the wrong way and blocks the airway.
A hole lets a little air squeeze past, buying precious time to get help.
So pen makers vent their caps, and the safety standard ISO 11540 requires air to be able to pass through.
BIC's classic Cristal has one right at the tip of its cap.
And the tiny hole in the pen's body? Different job. It keeps air pressure even, so the ink keeps flowing.

Fact notes: the hole in a ballpoint cap is a safety vent — if a cap is inhaled or swallowed and lodges in the airway, the hole lets
some air through. ISO 11540 (caps for writing and marking instruments, first published 1993) requires caps to allow a minimum airflow
(or to be large enough not to be swallowed). BIC states its Cristal cap is vented for this reason. The separate small hole in a pen's
barrel equalises air pressure so the ink can flow (and doesn't leak with pressure changes). "Not to keep the ink fresh" is the
common misconception the opening addresses.
