Every morning, millions of American students stand, face the flag, and say the Pledge of Allegiance.
But why?
It started in 1892.
A magazine was selling flags to schools,
and a minister named Francis Bellamy wrote a short pledge for Columbus Day.
The goal: help unite a country full of new immigrants.
At first, students saluted with one arm stretched out stiff.
During World War Two, that looked too much like the Nazi salute, so in 1942 it became a hand over the heart.
In 1954, the words "under God" were added.
And here's the twist: since 1943, no student can be forced to say it.

Fact notes: written by Francis Bellamy (a Baptist minister) for The Youth's Companion's 1892 Columbus Day school celebration;
the magazine sold flags to schools. Raised-arm "Bellamy salute" replaced by hand-over-heart when Congress amended the Flag Code
in December 1942. "Under God" added by Congress in 1954. West Virginia State Board of Education v. Barnette (1943): public-school
students cannot be compelled to salute the flag or say the pledge.
