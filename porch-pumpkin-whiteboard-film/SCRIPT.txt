Why do Americans put pumpkins on their doorsteps?
It started with turnips.
In Ireland and Scotland, people carved scary faces into turnips
and set them out to scare away wandering spirits on Halloween night.
Irish immigrants brought the custom to America in the 1800s.
But here, pumpkins were everywhere: bigger, softer, and much easier to carve.
Today, a glowing pumpkin on the porch sends a simple message:
trick-or-treaters welcome.
And the uncarved ones? They just say fall is here.
