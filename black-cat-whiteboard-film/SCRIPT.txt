You'd never cross a black cat's path.
But in Scotland, a black cat at your door is good luck.
So why is yours “cursed”?
Blame medieval Europe.
Black cats got tangled up with witchcraft, seen as witches' helpers, or even witches in disguise.
Some historians point to a papal letter from 1233 that linked cats to the devil, helping spread fear of them across Europe.
A woman living alone with a black cat could be suspected of being a witch.
And cats being independent didn't help their reputation.
Those superstitions sailed to America and stuck, right onto every Halloween decoration.
So black cats aren't unlucky.
They just had terrible PR in medieval Europe.

Fact notes
- In Scottish (and parts of British) folklore a strange black cat arriving at your door is a sign of prosperity / good luck.
- Medieval European belief linked black cats with witches, as familiars (helpers) or witches who had shape-shifted.
- "Vox in Rama", a papal bull of Pope Gregory IX (1233), describes a heretical rite involving a black cat; some historians link it to
  the persecution of cats, though how far it actually spread anti-cat feeling is debated (hence "some historians").
- Women living alone with cats were common targets of witchcraft accusations in the early modern period.
- These beliefs travelled to colonial America (e.g. the Salem era) and black cats became a standard Halloween image.
