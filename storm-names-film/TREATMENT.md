# Why do hurricanes get human names? — treatment

Whiteboard Explainer · 40 s · 1080×1920 (9:16) · English, warm male narrator (Kokoro `am_michael`) · subtitles burned in + `.srt`.
No speech-to-text check (Hugging Face blocked here); word timings come from pauses in the voice (`tools/align.py`).

## Three candidate structures
1. **A proof**: "names save lives" at the top, reasons boxed underneath. Clear, but no history, and history is half the answer.
2. **A debate**: numbers vs names on two halves. Good for the hook only; it runs out after 10 s.
3. **Problem, then a timeline ridden downward (chosen)**: two storms labelled by coordinates (confusing) → wiped and renamed → the pen draws a timeline straight down the tall board and the camera rides it through four eras → the last era's list loses one name for good → pull back to the whole board. A vertical frame suits a vertical timeline.

## Logline and arc
Coordinates are forgettable, so storms got names; the way we name them changed four times, and the worst names leave the list forever.
- Setup (0–7 s): two blue spirals, "15°N 62°W" / "16°N 59°W", a question mark.
- Turn (7.5–13 s): the eraser wipes the numbers; "Ana" and "Bill" in orange.
- Ride (13–33 s): saints' days (San Felipe) → 1950s radio alphabet (Able, Baker, Charlie) → 1953 women only → 1979 alternating → today, six lists in rotation.
- Ending (33.7–40.6 s): a beat of silence; "Katrina" written on the top list, struck out, the magnet drops on it ("retired"); pull back past the board edge to the whole board.

## Colours

## Hero magnet
An orange pin magnet is "now": it drops on each era's tick and hops down the timeline; at the end it leaves the timeline and pins "Katrina" — retired.
