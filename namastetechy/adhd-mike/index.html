This is Mike.
Mike sat down to answer one email.
Three hours later…
he's alphabetizing his spice rack.
And he still hasn't answered the email.
Sound familiar?
Let's look inside Mike's brain.
Picture Mike's mind as a control room.
In most brains, there's a manager deciding what's important.
In an ADHD brain, that manager is…
kind of on vacation.
ADHD affects focus, planning, and impulse control.
It's linked to how the brain handles dopamine, the chemical that says "this is worth doing."
Boring task?
Very little dopamine.
So the brain goes looking for something more exciting.
That's why Mike loses track of time.
"Five minutes" becomes an hour.
He walks into a room and forgets why.
He knows the bill is due…
he just can't make himself start.
It's not laziness.
His brain is simply wired differently.
And feelings?
They hit harder.
A small criticism can feel like a punch.
Frustration shows up fast.
Many people with ADHD say this is the hardest part, and the least talked about.
But here's the twist.
When something grabs Mike's interest, he locks in.
It's called hyperfocus.
Hours fly by.
Lots of people with ADHD are creative, energetic, and great at thinking on their feet.
So the lesson?
An ADHD brain isn't broken.
It's different.
Small steps, timers, and the right support can change everything.
And if this sounds like you, talk to a doctor.
See you next time.
